Interview type: Provisional interview notes based on current understanding of operations

Questions and Responses

1. How are fish sales currently recorded?

Fish sales are recorded manually. The records are used to keep track of the fish sold and the money received from customers.

2. What information is recorded when fish are sold?

The important information includes the date of sale, type of fish sold, quantity, selling price and total amount received.

3. How do you know the quantity of fish available for sale?

The available quantity is determined by checking the fish harvested from the pond and the quantities already sold.

4. How are fresh fish and smoked fish recorded?

They are treated as different products because some fish are sold fresh while others are smoked before being sold. Their quantities and sales need to be tracked separately.

5. What happens when fish are harvested?

The harvested fish are prepared for sale. Some are sold fresh, while others are taken for smoking. The quantities should be recorded to keep track of the available stock.

6. What challenges can occur when recording fish sales manually?

Manual records can be difficult to update and maintain. It may also take time to calculate total sales and determine the remaining quantities.

7. How do you calculate the total money received from fish sales?

The total is calculated using the quantity sold and the selling price. The amounts from different sales are added together to determine the total revenue.

8. What would you like a computerized system to help you do?

The system should make it easier to record fish sales, monitor available quantities, distinguish fresh fish from smoked fish and generate sales reports.

Key Findings

- Fish sales records need to be properly maintained.
- Fresh and smoked fish should be tracked separately.
- Harvested quantities and sold quantities should be recorded.
- The system should calculate sales totals automatically.
- Sales reports should be available when needed.

Interview 2: Laboratory / Production Worker

Stakeholder: Food Production Worker
Date: [Insert date if an interview is later conducted]
Interview type: Provisional interview notes based on current understanding of operations

Questions and Responses

1. What activities are carried out in the production laboratory?

The laboratory is responsible for preparing products such as tea, pepper and other food products produced by JAOF FOODS.

2. What materials are needed for production?

Different raw materials and ingredients are required depending on the product being prepared. These materials need to be available before production can take place.

3. How do you know whether enough raw materials are available?

The available materials are checked before production. If some materials are running low, they need to be identified and replaced.

4. How is the quantity of finished products determined?

The quantity is determined after production by counting or measuring the finished products.

5. What happens when raw materials are insufficient?

Production may be delayed or the quantity of products that can be produced may be reduced until the required materials are available.

6. How are finished products monitored after production?

The finished products need to be counted and recorded so that the business knows what is available for sale.

7. What difficulties can arise when managing production and stock manually?

It can be difficult to keep accurate records of materials used, products manufactured and remaining stock. Records may also become outdated if they are not updated regularly.

8. What would make a computerized system useful in the production laboratory?

It would be useful to record raw materials, monitor their quantities, record production activities and automatically update the available quantities of finished products.

Key Findings

- Raw materials and ingredients need to be monitored.
- Production activities should be recorded.
- The quantity of finished products should be tracked.
- The system should help identify insufficient raw materials.
- Finished product quantities should be updated after production.
- Production and inventory records should be accessible for reporting.

---

Overall Interview Findings

The two operational areas have related inventory management needs. Fish sales require proper tracking of harvested fish, fresh fish, smoked fish and sales. Food production requires monitoring raw materials, production activities and finished products.

The proposed system should therefore provide inventory management, production recording, fish stock management, sales recording and report generation.
